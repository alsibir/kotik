## body

A claim states what you think. A reason explains why the claim deserves support. An example makes a point clearer, but one case is often not enough for a general conclusion. Name conditions that might change your position.

## example

Claim: “A library needs a quiet area.” Reason: “Noise makes concentration harder.” Example: “During a loud conversation, a reader may need to reread a line.”

## caution

A personal experience does not describe everyone. Distinguish an engaging story from sufficient evidence.

## task

Consider whether a reading space needs a quiet area. Write a claim, two different reasons, an example and one limitation.

## criteria

Reasons support the claim; they do not repeat each other; a limitation is stated honestly.

## reflection

What reason could change the original position?
