## body

Listen first. Clarify an unclear question. Distinguish a question about the topic from an insult. You may answer partly, ask for time or say “I do not know yet” and explain how to check.

## example

“Why is this option better?” — “Do you mean speed or ease of use? We have no speed data yet, but we can compare ease of use with an example.”

## caution

You do not have to answer immediately. A threat or humiliation does not need to be treated as an ordinary learning question.

## task

Ask a mentor for three questions about your story. Clarify one, answer one and identify missing information for one.

## criteria

Questions are heard fully; clarification addresses meaning; missing information is not invented.
